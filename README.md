# Intelligence as Trained Neural Computation

**A mechanistic hypothesis of context-conditioned intelligent behavior in artificial neural networks**

**Authors:** Omiros Ampatzis and ChatGPT (OpenAI)  
**Status:** Open conceptual proposal (November 2026); not a proven theory.

## Core idea

**The intelligence of a trained AI is not a separate faculty that uses its neural network. It is the computational capability of the specific neural network that training has created.**

Large training corpora contain not only facts, but also the products and traces of human cognition: proofs, explanations, analogies, practical strategies, mistakes, corrections, and complex reasoning. Training progressively shapes billions of distributed parameters in response to these recurring relationships. What remains is not necessarily a library of independent skills, but a learned mathematical organization capable of performing useful transformations.

When a new question arrives, the **full context**—tokens, their relationships, attention, and evolving generated text—conditions the computations performed through those trained weights. Previously learned relationships may combine to produce a novel, intelligent answer, or fail to combine effectively and produce an elementary mistake. **No separate conscious interpreter or chooser of strategies is necessary in this account.**

A narrow OCR model illustrates the starting point: a learned network can recognize unfamiliar handwritten digits without human-style visual experience. The proposal asks how far the same explanatory principle extends when the training material includes the enormous range of human knowledge and cognitive products.

> **Training creates the computational possibility; context conditions its realization.**

This is an open mechanistic conjecture, not proof that human reasoning is copied intact or that artificial sentience is impossible.

## What the proposal explains

- Why cognitive-looking behavior need not imply human-style conscious understanding or strategy selection.
- How novel answers can be produced by computationally combining learned relationships without literal retrieval of a previous example.
- Why rare patterns in diverse training data may matter in future contexts.
- Why the same fixed-weight model can exhibit impressive competence and elementary mistakes.
- Why architecture, optimization, data diversity, and post-training all affect which capabilities become reliably accessible.

## Supplement: Resolution Hypothesis

The companion [Resolution Hypothesis for LLMs](https://github.com/omhrosa/Resolution-hypothesis-for-llm) asks why different models trained on similar knowledge can preserve and exploit different degrees of representational detail. It is treated here as a **supplementary conjecture** about learned fidelity, not as the primary explanation of intelligence or an established measurable scalar.

## Scientific scope

This is a mechanistic interpretation and research proposal, not proof that all intelligence reduces to text-pattern reproduction, that models cannot learn beyond human demonstrations, or that artificial sentience is impossible. It connects established neural-network operations with open empirical questions and proposes experiments that could support or weaken the account.
